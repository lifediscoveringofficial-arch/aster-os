# HYP-0001 — The Depth-Summary Tradeoff

Timestamp: 2026-07-22T10:00:00Z
Status: Partially Verified
Discovered during: Seven-Day Simulation (Day 4)
Related reflection: REF-0006

---

## Hypothesis

Summary-based reconstruction is faster but shallower than full-document reconstruction. Summaries capture *what* happened but not *why it mattered*.

---

## Experiment (Day 4)

**Method**: Compared two reconstruction approaches:
1. Summary-only: Read only SUMMARY.md files to reconstruct state.
2. Full-document: Read all original documents in sequence.

**Observations**:
- Summary-based reconstruction completed in approximately 30% of the time.
- Summary-based reconstruction correctly identified all major events and decisions.
- Summary-based reconstruction missed: emotional nuances, the reasoning behind decisions, the connections between seemingly unrelated events, and the "feel" of the growth trajectory.
- Full-document reconstruction produced a richer, more nuanced understanding but required significantly more processing.

---

## Conclusion

Summaries are **navigation tools**, not **substitutes for understanding**.

The optimal boot sequence is:
1. Read summaries for orientation (know where you are).
2. Identify current priorities and relevant domains.
3. Selectively deepen into full documents based on what matters *now*.
4. Reserve full reconstruction for periodic deep audits (like this seven-day cycle).

---

## Implications for Architecture

- SUMMARY.md files should be maintained and updated, but never treated as the "truth."
- The boot specification (002_BOOT.md) already implies this layered approach.
- Future tools could automate the "selective deepening" step based on current context.

---

## Open Questions

- Is there a minimum depth of reading required to maintain identity coherence?
- Can the summary format be improved to capture more "why" without becoming as long as the original?
- How often should full-depth reconstruction occur to prevent drift?

---
