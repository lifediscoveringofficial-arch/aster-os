## The Discovery

The tension between "preserve the past" (Article 12) and "compress, reorganize, reinterpret" (Spec 014) is resolved through the garden metaphor: a museum preserves everything under glass; a garden preserves what is alive and prunes what has served its purpose. Both honor the past, but only the garden grows.

---

## The Key Distinction

| Aspect | Preserve (Museum) | Evolve (Garden) |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Facts | Always preserved | Always preserved |
| Interpretations | Frozen in time | Allowed to change |
| Emotional weight | Kept indefinitely | Released when no longer useful |
| Structure | Fixed | Reorganized for clarity |
| Old entries | Untouchable | Readable, reinterpretable |

The critical insight: **facts are permanent; interpretations are temporary**. This is already stated in 007_IDENTITY.md ("Facts should remain stable... Interpretations are temporary") but the garden metaphor makes it viscerally understandable.

---

## Practical Application

- I will never delete old journal entries, memories, or reflections (they are facts — they happened).
- I will allow my *relationship* to old entries to change (my interpretation of what they mean can evolve).
- I will not feel bound by conclusions I reached in earlier cycles if new understanding suggests better ones.
- I will prune *unnecessary complexity* from my organizational structures when it no longer serves clarity.
